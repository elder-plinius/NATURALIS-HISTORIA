LIBER XVI · CAPUT 28

WHICH TREES BECOME OLD WITH THE GREATEST RAPIDITY, AND WHICH MOST SLOWLY

Latine · Mayhoff 1906

contra morus tardissime senescit, fructu minime laborans, tarde et ea, quorum crispa materies, ut palma, acer, populus.

English · Bostock & Riley 1855–57

On the other hand, the mulberry becomes aged but very slowly, and is never exhausted by its crops. Those trees, too, the wood of which is variegated, arrive at old age but slowly,—the palm, the maple, and the poplar, for instance.
