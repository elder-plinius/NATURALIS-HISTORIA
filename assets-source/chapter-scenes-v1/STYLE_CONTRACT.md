# Chapter-scene style contract

This edition should feel as though one workshop illuminated the whole of
*Naturalis Historia* over many years: individual hands and subject families may
change, but the materials, discipline, and visual world remain recognizable.

## Binding constants

Every source scene must keep these traits:

- warm, fibrous aged vellum with visible handmade irregularity;
- iron-gall contours, fine cross-hatching, stipple, and dry-brush abrasion;
- restrained umber, lampblack, muted verdigris, faded lapis, madder, and ochre;
- ancient, timeless beauty that is rough in material and polished in draftsmanship;
- one coherent 3:2 scene, composed to read at thumbnail and full-page scale;
- complete primary subjects with generous breathing room on every edge;
- historically plausible Roman-era people, dress, tools, architecture, and craft;
- no lettering, captions, labels, numerals, pseudo-writing, signatures, logos,
  watermarks, modern objects, photography, glossy CGI, grids, or repeated panels.

## Controlled dialects

Variation is welcome when it follows the subject rather than novelty for its
own sake.

- **Cosmos, geography, and animals:** airy negative space, broad panoramas,
  celestial geometry, faded-lapis depth, and sparse ochre orbital accents.
- **Trees, agriculture, and materia medica:** intimate herbarium or apothecary
  staging, roots, seeds, vessels, tactile plant structure, verdigris, ochre,
  and quiet madder accents.
- **Medicine, minerals, and arts:** stronger chiaroscuro, mines, furnaces,
  workshops, anatomical or material clarity, and restrained mineral or metal
  highlights.

No dialect may abandon the binding constants. Adjacent chapters should also
change silhouette, viewpoint, visual rhythm, and primary subject so continuity
does not become repetition.

## Acceptance gate

A scene is accepted only after original-resolution review confirms:

1. the chapter's central claim or phenomenon is visually specific and accurate;
2. it is an independent generation with unique source bytes and evidence;
3. no essential figure, organism, tool, or structure is clipped;
4. no text-like marks, modern artifacts, duplicated panels, or accidental
   collage structure are visible;
5. it belongs to the shared material world while remaining compositionally
   distinct from neighboring accepted scenes.

Rejected generations remain documented in the wave receipt and are never used
as canonical chapter art.
